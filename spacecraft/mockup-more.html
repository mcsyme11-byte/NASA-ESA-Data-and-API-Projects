Spacecraft map
Mock-up for approval (Nov 14, 2026). A still picture of the spacecraft map with the planned additions; nothing here works yet, and the route drawing is a sketch, not real positions. The numbered parts:
  1. Show: chips that switch on past missions and the Earth-orbit fleet (relay and weather satellites). Active craft stay on by default. Added under the Kind chips; nothing on the page switches groups of craft today.
  2. Mission: a new chip row for big missions, small missions, satellites (anything circling Earth) and crewed missions. It works together with Agency and Kind, like those rows do now.
  3. The list marks past missions in grey with their years ("1997–2017") and how they ended in a word.
  4. The card of a past mission gets a short "How it ended" paragraph in place of the live figures.
  5. Replay its route: a play bar under the map draws the route from launch to the end, with the date, a slider to scrub and the speed buttons the map already has. It replaces "Show its path" for past missions.

What the switches add

Past missions (about 40, all with routes in NASA/JPL Horizons): Pioneer 10 and 11, Voyager-era and later deep-space craft such as Galileo, Cassini, Ulysses, NEAR Shoemaker, Stardust, Deep Impact, MESSENGER, Rosetta, Dawn, Kepler, Spitzer, Herschel, Planck, Venus Express, Akatsuki, Spirit, Opportunity, Phoenix, InSight, MAVEN, Mars Global Surveyor, LCROSS, LADEE, GRAIL, DART, LICIACube, OSIRIS-REx's sample capsule, Artemis I and Artemis II, Swift, Gaia and others. Each gets its years, how it ended in a short paragraph, and its route to replay.

Earth-orbit fleet (switched off by default, to keep Earth orbit readable): NASA's TDRS relay satellites, the older GOES and Meteosat weather satellites and other working relay and weather satellites with published orbits.

Mission sizes: "Big" means the agencies' large flagship-class missions (for example Webb, Hubble, Cassini, Europa Clipper, the space stations); "Small" means small satellites and CubeSats (for example CAPSTONE, MarCO, Pandora). Each craft's size class is recorded in the catalogue and shown on its card.
