What the record shows

Curiosity, sols 1–4843; Perseverance, sols 1–1859

The air thickens and thins with the seasons. Each Mars year, the air pressure at Gale Crater falls and rises again by about a fifth. It is thinnest around Ls 140–150°, in southern winter, when carbon dioxide freezes out of the air onto the south polar cap: about 745 Pa at the landing site's height. It is thickest around Ls 250°, when that ice turns back to gas: about 925 Pa. A smaller rise and fall in between comes from the northern cap. Air pressure through the year

The cycle repeats almost exactly, year after year. Lined up by season and corrected for the rover's climb, the Mars Years usually lie within about 6 Pa of each other, under 1 %. Even in Mars Year 34 (2018), when dust covered the whole planet, the pressure kept to its usual line. The dust showed in the sunlight instead: in that season the midday ultraviolet fell to about a quarter of its usual level, about 5 W/m² against 19–21 in other years. Air pressure through the year, The 2018 dust storm

A sol swings by about 60 °C. On an average sol the air's low and high are about 60 °C apart. It is coldest just before sunrise, about −72 °C at 05:00, and warmest in mid-afternoon, about −20 °C at 15:00. The ground swings further, about 83 °C, and warms first, peaking around 13:00. One sol

Afternoons are warmest in southern spring and coolest in southern autumn. Gale is just south of the equator. Daily highs average about −4 °C around Ls 180–210° and about −30 °C around Ls 60–90°, when Mars is farthest from the Sun. The nights change less: about −70 °C against −81 °C. Season and time of day, Daily highs and lows

Humidity peaks in the coldest hour. Cold air holds less water, so the relative humidity is highest just before dawn, between 04:00 and 06:00 on most sols. Through the year it is highest around Ls 90–120°, about 50 %, and lowest around Ls 210–240°, about 9 % (preliminary data). Humidity at night

Two craters: the same pressure rhythm, opposite humid seasons. Perseverance at Jezero Crater sees the same seasonal rise and fall of pressure at the same times, but with about 85–105 Pa less, roughly a tenth less, because Jezero's floor lies higher than Gale's. Their most humid nights come at opposite times of year, each in its own winter: Gale, just south of the equator, around Ls 90–150°; Jezero, 18° north, around Ls 270–300°. Height matters too: Curiosity has climbed about 990 m up Mount Sharp, which alone lowers its pressure by about 8–9 %. Air pressure and humidity at two sites, The rovers' climbs

The 2018 dust storm

Mars Year 34, from Curiosity's readings

NASA's Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter first saw the storm on May 30, 2018; by June 20 it had grown into a dust storm that covered the whole planet, the first since 2007. Curiosity's sunlight readings show it reaching Gale Crater in the second week of June (sols 2075–2085). Each chart sets that year, Mars Year 34, against the same season in Curiosity's other Mars Years.

Above the surface

ESA's Mars Express, from near the ground to about 45 km up

The rovers measure the air where they stand. ESA's Mars Express orbiter measures it from above: as the orbiter passes behind Mars, its radio signal to Earth crosses the atmosphere and bends, and the bending gives the temperature and pressure at each height. Each pass is one profile, at one place and one time.
