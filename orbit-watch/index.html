Why the totals differ

NASA's Orbital Debris Program Office counts the objects in the public United States catalogue. ESA's Space Debris Office counts every object that surveillance networks follow regularly, including some that are not in the public catalogue.

Both are right: they count different lists, on different dates. Every number on these pages says which list it comes from.

Tracked

Objects bigger than about 10 cm in low orbit (a softball) can be followed by radar; each has a known orbit and an owner.

Estimated

Smaller pieces cannot be followed one by one. Their numbers come from models built on radar sweeps and on the pits found in surfaces brought back from space.

Fast

In low orbit everything moves at 7 to 8 km/s, and two objects meet at about 10 km/s on average, up to about 15 km/s (NASA). At that speed a 1 cm piece of aluminium hits as hard as a 1-tonne car at about 40 km/h.
